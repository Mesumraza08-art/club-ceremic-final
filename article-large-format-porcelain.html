How to choose large-format porcelain

24×48 and 32×64 formats change how a room feels. Here is how to pick the size, finish and layout.

Tile Guides, 6 min read, Club Ceramic

Large-format porcelain is the biggest shift in tile in a decade. Fewer joints make a floor read as one surface, walls look like slabs, and small rooms feel larger. The trade-off is that big tiles ask more of the room and the installer.

Start with the room, not the tile

A 32×64 tile in a powder room means almost every piece is cut, which wastes material and breaks the effect. As a rough guide, 24×48 suits most kitchens, bathrooms and living areas, and 32×64 comes into its own on long walls, open floors and showers where you want as few joints as possible.

Polished or matt

Glossy porcelain reflects light and deepens marble veining; it is beautiful on walls and in living spaces. Matt and stone finishes are calmer and grip better underfoot, which matters in bathrooms, entries and kitchens. Many lines come in both, so you can run matt on the floor and polished on the wall.

Plan the layout before you order

Decide where the first full tile lands, how the joints meet doors and windows, and whether veining should flow across pieces. Order from the same lot, add 10 percent for cuts on a straight lay, and ask the showroom for the matching adhesive and levelling system.
